# HELIX published-data audit — historical 18.1 snapshot

For the current 18.2 extraction scope, see PED-DEEP-REVIEW.md. The eight-study description below records the previous release, not the current 16-extraction workbench.

## Scope and independence

Educational review of published aggregate results: selected primary abstracts, indexed publisher results, full Nass article/methods/tables, and FDA labels. No participant-level dataset was available. This is not a raw-data reanalysis, systematic review, clinical advice, trial protocol or risk-adjusted PED ranking. Access failures were not bypassed; the app names abstract-only/indexed access. Missing values were not invented.

Eight detailed study cards refresh GH, adult testosterone, EPO, tesamorelin, MK-677, LGD-4033, GH collagen metabolism and CJC-1295 DAC. Five anchor cards illustrate different inferential problems; order is editorial, not a drug preference. The retained 26-compound library/19 trial extracts have their own earlier review date, including BPC-157/TB-500, pediatric mecasermin, follistatin, myostatin/activin, AOD, MOTS-c, humanin, GDF-15, insulin and supplement comparators. They were not all newly reverified.

## Five anchor findings — read in the app

| Study | What it teaches | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Bhasin 1996 adult testosterone | Gym strength and FFM can increase together | Quoted active-arm change is not the incremental drug effect; no teenage football prediction |
| Meinhardt 2010 GH | A measured cycling-work signal can coexist with null strength/jump results | Cycling work is not a timed running sprint; water is not contractile muscle |
| Heuberger 2017 EPO | Lab aerobic outcomes and road-race uncertainty can diverge | Neither universal endurance efficacy nor RB/LB acceleration follows |
| Falutz 2007 tesamorelin | Disease-specific visceral-fat and metabolic results | General teenage recomp or performance |
| Nass 2008 MK-677 | FFM and hormones can rise without functional improvement | Extra mass is not demonstrated athletic benefit |

Quantitative findings, comparator basis, intervals and primary links appear directly beside each table. The app's MK arithmetic example subtracts published arm changes only; it does not reconstruct an adjusted treatment model or manufacture an interval from two arm intervals.

## SEED audit logic

**Structural evidence:** disease treatment, healthy-adult physiology and direct athletic outcomes are separate evidence scopes. A randomized biomarker trial has good internal design for that biomarker but no direct running evidence. A single direct trial is not replicated Level 1 evidence. Missing trial registration/details are not fabricated.

**Effect pathway:** exogenous GH, direct IGF-1, pituitary GHRH agonism and ghrelin-receptor stimulation have different upstream/direct effects. Androgen receptor effects cannot be borrowed by each SARM or peptide. Myostatin/activin inhibition and GDF-15 inhibition are different pathways; gene therapy is not a marketed protein formulation.

**Emergent risks:** glucose intolerance versus hypoglycemia are not balancing treatments. Shared endocrine exposure may overlap without demonstrated additional benefit. Monitoring detects some problems, not all developmental or delayed harm. There is no evidence-backed assurance of “a small amount of harm” for enhancement in a normally developing adolescent.

**Decision logic:** assign an outcome-specific finding; do not sum unlike endpoints into an impact score. Separate supported adult gym effects, plausible mechanisms, untested transfer and safety uncertainty. Research pages do not affect clinical clearance, readiness, workout prescriptions or the ordinary training pilot.

## Connective tissue, recovery and injury

Tissue turnover is not mature-tendon load capacity. Pain scores, structural imaging, strength restoration, recurrence and safe return to collision are distinct outcomes. Animal injury work and human cell observations do not fill missing controlled athletic endpoints. Full-length thymosin-beta-4 ocular trials cannot validate an injected TB-500 fragment. GH rehabilitation and uninjured collagen experiments must retain different populations and endpoints.

## Adolescents and regulation

The FDA tesamorelin label does not establish pediatric safety/effectiveness and identifies potential excessive linear growth with open growth plates. Mecasermin's specific pediatric disease indication does not validate enhancement; its label documents severe hypoglycemia, including seizures. These labels also raise growth/orthopedic and proliferative considerations requiring disease-specific specialist interpretation—not a self-monitoring checklist that makes use safe.

## Better unanswered questions

Would any exact intervention improve running acceleration, maximum velocity, repeated-running sprint ability, RFD or jump outputs beyond an otherwise complete supervised training program? Would benefits survive the change in total mass? Are reported changes contractile tissue, extracellular water or connective tissue? What are adolescent developmental and long-term adverse outcomes? Can clinically meaningful injury return and recurrence be demonstrated independently of pain relief? How much reporting/selective-publication bias affects apparent benefits?

Ordinary training records can answer narrower questions about that athlete's measured progression. They cannot isolate drug efficacy from puberty, training, diet, expectancy and water shifts. Drug research requires a legitimate investigational framework with qualified oversight, independent ethics review, consent/assent and safety governance. The app creates no PED self-experiment.
