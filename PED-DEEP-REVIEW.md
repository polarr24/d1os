Reading method: inspect published aggregate results, methods and tables where accessible; retain primary abstracts/indexed publisher results when full access is blocked. No participant-level dataset was supplied. No independent meta-analysis, exhaustive global evidence sweep or estimate of an individual athlete's drug response was performed. Source access and table/abstract locations are attached to the cards. Study dosing/administration is not reproduced.

The five anchor section is an editorial selection of informative findings—not a most-impactful or safest-PED ranking. The data do not establish little-harm enhancement for healthy adolescents.

## Five inference problems a useful app must prevent

1. **Outcome substitution.** A hormone response is not speed. Wingate cycling work is not timed running. Stair function is not explosive field transfer. A pain score is not tendon strength.
2. **Population substitution.** Children with severe endocrine disease, adults with HIV, older adults, post-surgical patients and normally developing teenage athletes differ substantially.
3. **Compartment substitution.** DXA lean mass includes nonfat tissues and hydration influences. Muscle volume, whole-body FFM and myofibrillar synthesis answer different questions.
4. **Comparator substitution.** Active-arm change is not extra benefit over a training-only comparator. Adjusted observational associations are not randomized drug effects.
5. **Safety substitution.** No events observed is not no possible risk. Long-term follow-up in indicated adult treatment is not safety for different exposure, products, populations or developmental stages.

## Reading conflicting evidence

“Contradiction” should trigger examination, not cherry-picking. Compare indication, age, sex, baseline health, exact molecule/formulation, exposure, duration, comparator, training, endpoint, analysis and missing data. It is possible for a clinical intervention to change one endpoint while another does not improve. Null findings from a small study do not demonstrate equivalence; a secondary positive does not erase a primary null.

A single favorable result cannot supply a universal response rate. Adult observed changes cannot be extrapolated as a teenage expectation. Biomarkers can support biological activity without supporting performance. No directly measured running-sprint improvement is identified in these selected extractions; that statement is bounded to this collection, not a claim of an exhaustive worldwide search.

## Harm interpretation

The new harms view attaches denominators, follow-up context and limits to official-label observations. It does not rank products by these percentages. Trials cannot be compared as if the same adverse-event collection, population and exposure were used. Some categories overlap. Ever having an event over several years is not annual incidence. Rounded percentages must not be converted into purported exact counts. Hazard ratios are time-to-event measures, not simple percentage differences.

The zero-event lesson is independently calculated binomial arithmetic: an exact one-sided 95% upper bound under stated assumptions. It is not a confidence interval for an individual or the efficacy of a product. Selection, incomplete event ascertainment and delayed harm restrict even that illustrative calculation.

The regulatory view links the FDA's updated testosterone context, including June 2026 changes, rather than assuming older label language remains current. This does not imply authorization for adolescent enhancement. WADA 2026 remains separate from physiological efficacy; verify the edition applicable to the actual competition date and sport. EMA/worldwide authorization has not been comprehensively audited.

## Clinical and developmental questions

Take redacted papers/claims to an independent pediatric sports-medicine or endocrinology specialist. Ask what diagnosed condition, exact product and clinical endpoint justify an intervention; how pubertal stage, age-specific ranges and growth plates affect interpretation; what glucose, growth, orthopedic, reproductive, cardiovascular and proliferative uncertainties remain; and who independently oversees adverse-event decisions. An entered clinician note is not authenticated medical clearance in this app.

No dose, cycle, injection, sourcing, stack, drug-experiment design or “safe enough” threshold is provided. The app's ordinary training pilot remains limited to familiar rest or movement order. Drug research is not personal before/after training tracking; it requires legitimate investigational governance, qualified clinical oversight, independent ethics review, consent/assent and safety monitoring.

## Supported, plausible and unestablished

**Supported within narrow populations:** some adult gym effects, some disease composition/growth outcomes, some laboratory endurance/function findings, and biological activity of GH-axis agents.

**Plausible but not supplied by mechanisms alone:** additional athletic transfer, useful contractile hypertrophy, improved training recovery or connective-tissue capacity.

**Not established for this athlete:** a low-harm PED choice, healthy-adolescent enhancement safety, faster football acceleration, better decision-making, a guaranteed muscle response, tendon-healing time or recruiting probability. Better controlled direct endpoint studies—not a personal challenge video—would be needed to answer those questions.
